You don't need to read Greek or Latin to use these pages. The English sits right under each original, and the notes say where a word is disputed and why.

## How to read these pages

**How far each text was checked.** Each passage carries a letter. **V** means the text was compared with an edition, or a scan of one. **S** means it was checked against a scholar's quotation of it, because no edition could be reached online. **R** means it wasn't checked. Each scholar's reading carries the same kind of note: read in their own words, taken from a reliable report, or a standard attribution not yet checked.

**Translations.** Older translations whose copyright has run out, such as the nineteenth-century *Ante-Nicene Fathers* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers* (ANF and NPNF), are quoted and named. Everywhere else the English is our own close translation, and the standard published translation is named so you can compare.

**Editions.** A critical edition is a scholar's text built from the surviving manuscripts. The abbreviations you'll meet most are: **ACO**, the acts of the councils (Schwartz, Straub, Riedinger); **CCSL** and **CCSG**, the Corpus Christianorum Latin and Greek series; **CSEL**, the Vienna Corpus of Latin church writers; **GCS**, the Berlin series of Greek Christian writers; **GNO**, the Leiden edition of Gregory of Nyssa; **PG** and **PL**, Migne's nineteenth-century Greek and Latin patrologies; **PO**, Patrologia Orientalis; **SC**, Sources Chrétiennes. Many of the texts here were checked against the open digital editions of the First1KGreek and Open Greek and Latin projects.
