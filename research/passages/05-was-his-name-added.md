# Was Origen's name added later?

Some scholars have argued that Origen's name was slipped into the condemnations after the fact. The argument is almost entirely about one list, the eleventh anathema of the council of 553. This file goes through every council and document that names him and asks how secure the name is in each.

## What these texts show

Origen's name is in canon 11 of the Second Council of Constantinople (553), in a list of heretics each of whom the council 'anathematizes' (anathema: a solemn curse that cuts someone off from the church), in every text of that canon that anyone in this survey reports, Latin and Greek, and no source reports a manuscript without it. Whether the name was in the text the bishops approved is a separate question, and the evidence does not settle it. The case that it was added later (Halloix 1648, Garnier 1675, Vincenzi 1860s; today Ramelli, Harding, Słomski, and Hart without giving evidence) is a probability argument, strongest in this form: the list reads as the heretics of the four earlier councils, in the order of those councils, and Origen is the one name none of them condemned; Justinian's own Confession of Faith of 551 has the same six names without him, which Hefele concedes; the popes who describe the council (Vigilius, Pelagius, Gregory the Great) speak only of the Three Chapters; and the Greek original of the Acts is lost, the Latin surviving in recensions that differ considerably and in two editions, in a book the sixth council (680-81) found had been tampered with. The case for the name is also cumulative: no manuscript lacking it is known; the Greek canon and the Latin agree, and the Latin was made 'in the immediate aftermath of the council'; Origen had already been condemned by name by Theophilus's synod (400), by Pope Anastasius and by Justinian's edict of 543, so the name adds no new man to the church's anathemas; Price explains the last place as the trace of a real, recent condemnation; the 551 text is a different document written before the push against Origenism; Vigilius's letter of 8 December 553 anathematizes no heresiarch by name except Theodore of Mopsuestia, so it has no list for Origen to be missing from; and Cyril of Scythopolis (c. 557), Evagrius Scholasticus (c. 593) and later councils took the council to have condemned him. In 1899 Diekamp wrote that by far most scholars held the name genuine, and Kimel (2026) still calls interpolation a minority view. What the evidence settles: Origen was condemned by name before 553 and again in conciliar texts from 649; the name is absent from the 551 Confession's list and from every one of the fifteen anathemas; the 680-81 inquiry concerned forged Monothelete letters, not Origen. What it does not settle: whether the name was in the council's own draft, was added by the emperor or the bishops at the council (Hefele's alternative, which counts as genuine), or was added afterwards; what the 'Homonoia' is; and what the critical edition's apparatus shows, which no one in this survey has seen. Nobody read has examined the Latin manuscripts for canon 11, on either side.

### 1. 400-401: Theophilus's synodal letter and Pope Anastasius condemn Origen by name

**Source:** Theophilus of Alexandria, Synodal letter to the bishops of Palestine and Cyprus (= Jerome, Ep. 92.1.4 and 92.2.1); Anastasius I, Letter to Simplicianus of Milan (= Jerome, Ep. 95); with the reply of the Jerusalem synod (= Jerome, Ep. 93) · **Date:** 400 (Theophilus's synod at Alexandria / Nitria); c. 400-401 (Anastasius)  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

These are the oldest synodal and papal condemnations of Origen, and nobody questions that his name is in them: it runs through both texts. They are the baseline against which the sixth-century cases look uncertain. The Jerusalem reply (Ep. 93) shows how even a friendly synod answered: it condemned the listed propositions and whoever preaches them, and left Origen's own name out of its anathema.

**Latin (Theophilus wrote in Greek; the letter survives in a Latin version preserved in Jerome's correspondence)**

> Theophilus, Ep. 92.1.4 (CSEL 55, p. 148): "et coram multis patribus monachorum, qui de tota paene Aegypto conuolauerunt, lecti sunt libri Origenis, in quibus inpio labore sudauit, et consensu omnium condemnati."
Ep. 92.2.1 (p. 149), after quoting propositions including "Christi regnum finietur aliquando, zabulus cunctis peccatorum sordibus liberatus aequo honore decorabitur et cum Christo subicietur": "obturauimus aures nostras et tam Origenem quam discipulos eius consona uoce damnauimus, ne et modicum fermentum totam massam corrumperet."
Anastasius, Ep. 95 (CSEL 55, p. 158): "ne dei populus per diuersas ecclesias Origenem legendo in magnas incurrat blasphemias ... illud, quicquid est fidei nostrae contrarium ab Origene quondam scriptum, indicauimus a nobis esse alienum atque punitum ... si qua alia sunt ab Origene exposita, cum suo auctore pariter a nobis scias esse damnata."
Reply of the bishops at Jerusalem (Eulogius, John and the others), Ep. 93 (CSEL 55, pp. 155-156): "neque enim audiuimus umquam docentes, quod Christi regnum aliquando sit terminandum ... neque, quod zabulus cunctis peccatorum uitiis liberatus dignitatem obtineat, quam habuit, antequam caderet ... quicumque haec praedicat, quae beatitudo tua damnanda significat ... et ipsi et dogmata eorum sint ecclesiae anathema cum Apollinare."

**English** (Own translation (Theophilus, Anastasius and the Jerusalem reply). The standard English of Anastasius's separate letter to John of Jerusalem is NPNF II.3 (public domain); I re-read its wording on the ccel.org page and the quoted phrases are right.)

> Theophilus: "In the presence of many fathers of the monks, who had flocked together from almost the whole of Egypt, the books of Origen, over which he sweated with impious labour, were read and condemned by the consent of all." After quoting propositions that Theophilus says stand in Origen's 'de principiis' and in a book 'de oratione' (among them that "the kingdom of Christ will one day come to an end, and the devil, freed from all the filth of sins, will be honoured equally and made subject together with Christ"): "we stopped our ears, and with one voice we condemned Origen as well as his disciples, so that a little leaven should not corrupt the whole lump."
Anastasius: "... lest the people of God in the various churches fall into great blasphemies by reading Origen ... whatever Origen once wrote that is contrary to our faith we have declared foreign to us and condemned ... and if there are other things set out by Origen, know that they, together with their author, are equally condemned by us."
Jerusalem reply (Ep. 93, own translation): "We have never heard anyone teaching that Christ's kingdom will at some time be ended ... or that the devil, freed from all the vices of sins, will obtain the dignity he had before he fell ... whoever preaches these things that your Blessedness marks out for condemnation ... let both they and their teachings be an anathema to the church, together with Apollinaris."

**Edition:** I. Hilberg, Sancti Eusebii Hieronymi Epistulae, CSEL 55 (Vienna 1912): Ep. 92 at pp. 147-155 (lines quoted at p. 148 and p. 149, ll. 1-10); Ep. 93 at pp. 155-156; Ep. 95 at pp. 157-158 (Ep. 92 runs pp. 147-155, confirmed from the page breaks and the next letter's heading). Line numbers are approximate: the first quotation is at the foot of p. 148 (about ll. 23-25).  
**Checked against:** Hilberg's CSEL 55 text (Open Greek and Latin csel-dev XML, with page breaks and apparatus), downloaded again and compared line by line: all quoted Latin matches, and pages 148, 149 and 158 are right. Ep. 93 read in the same file. The quoted phrases of Anastasius's letter to John of Jerusalem checked on the NPNF II.3 page (ccel.org). ([source 1](https://github.com/OpenGreekAndLatin/csel-dev), [source 2](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf203.vi.x.html), [source 3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGreekAndLatin/csel-dev/master/data/stoa0162/stoa004/stoa0162.stoa004.opp-lat1.xml))

**About the text:** Both letters rest on one late manuscript, Milan, Ambrosianus H 59 sup. (13th century), which Hilberg calls u and which lacks their titles. In Ep. 95 Hilberg marks a lacuna after "ut" and supplies "sint tuta"; neither touches Origen's name. The propositions, including the one about the devil, are Theophilus's report of what Origen's books say (he names 'de principiis' and a book 'de oratione'), not Origen's own words, and I did not check them against Origen's text. Origen denied teaching the devil's salvation in a letter that Rufinus quotes in De adulteratione librorum Origenis (section number not confirmed here). Ep. 92, 93 and 95 all rest on the same manuscript (Hilberg's apparatus cites only it and Vallarsi's edition). In Ep. 93 the Jerusalem bishops name Origen only to describe what Theophilus had told them ('Origenis dogmatibus pestifera quaedam uelint introducere'); in the lines quoted they anathematize the propositions and whoever preaches them, and do not themselves condemn Origen by name. Anastasius's letter to John of Jerusalem says he has 'neither formerly known, nor do I now seek to know' who Origen was, yet condemns his writings and cites a decree of the emperors against reading them (NPNF II.3).

**How scholars read it**

- **John of Jerusalem (ancient reader), as reported by Mark Harding**, Harding 2004, p. 164 (Westminster Handbook to Origen), as cited in Wikipedia, 'Origenist crises'. Refused Epiphanius's demand, made in the 390s during the first controversy and before the 400 synod, to condemn Origen. Wikipedia reports Harding's wording as: John refused 'because a person could not be retroactively condemned as a heretic after death'. (*objects to condemning Origen after his death*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origenist_crises))
- **Fr Aidan Kimel**, 'An Open Letter to Dr Cyril Jenkins: Was Origen Condemned for Teaching Universal Salvation?', Eclectic Orthodoxy, posted 20 Aug 2024 (revised 5 Sep 2024). Dates Theophilus's turn against Origen to 399 and says he acted 'primarily for political and pastoral reasons'. Says that 'neither Epiphanius nor Theophilus condemn Origen for teaching the final salvation of all human beings', and reads that as a sign that the restoration of all people was still within orthodox limits at the end of the fourth century. (*reads 399-400 as not a condemnation of universal salvation as such*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2024/08/20/an-open-letter-to-dr-cyril-jenkins-was-origen-condemned-for-teaching-universal-salvation/))
- **Dr Cyril Jenkins (as reported by Kimel)**, Ancient Faith interview, as quoted and answered in Kimel's open letter of 2024. As quoted by Kimel: the 'real condemnations of Origen' come 'from people like St Epiphanius', and 'the Orthodox Church has always accepted the condemnations of 553'. Kimel says Jenkins thereby approves the condemnations by Epiphanius and Theophilus. (*treats the early condemnations as real and binding*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2024/08/20/an-open-letter-to-dr-cyril-jenkins-was-origen-condemned-for-teaching-universal-salvation/))
- **Eulogius, John and the bishops at Jerusalem (ancient readers)**, Reply to Theophilus's synodal letter = Jerome, Ep. 93 (CSEL 55, pp. 155-156). They say they have never heard anyone teach that Christ's kingdom will end or that the devil will regain his old dignity, and they declare anyone who preaches such things, with their teachings, anathema to the church. They mention Origen only as the source Theophilus gave for these teachings. (*condemns the propositions and their teachers, not Origen by name*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGreekAndLatin/csel-dev/master/data/stoa0162/stoa004/stoa0162.stoa004.opp-lat1.xml))
- **Rufinus of Aquileia (ancient reader)**, De adulteratione librorum Origenis (c. 400); the Origen letter is quoted there (section not confirmed). Argued that heretics had falsified Origen's books, and published a letter of Origen's in which Origen complains that people attribute to him the teaching that the devil will be saved. (*Origen did not teach the devil's salvation; the books were tampered with*; standard attribution, not yet checked)
- **Jerome (ancient reader)**, Apologia contra Rufinum, book 2 (c. 402); sections not checked. Held that Origen's disclaimer in that letter did not clear him, because Jerome found the restoration of the devil taught in De principiis. (*Origen did teach the restoration of the devil*; standard attribution, not yet checked)


### 2. 543: the last anathema of Justinian's edict against Origen

**Source:** Justinian, Letter (edict) to Menas against Origen (Liber adversus Origenem), the closing anathema (counted as the tenth: nine numbered anathemas come first, and this one is unnumbered in NPNF and sits after the ninth in Migne) · **Date:** 543  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

This is the earliest surviving imperial text I know of that anathematizes Origen by name (Anastasius's letter of c. 400 mentions an earlier decree of the emperors against reading him, which I did not see), and it is the text that the 553 sources keep referring back to. The ninth anathema just before it is the edict's own condemnation of the end of punishment. Whether its Greek and Latin disagree about the name bears on how reliable the record is.

**Greek, with the Latin printed beside it in Migne**

> Greek (PG 86.1, col. 989), immediately after the ninth anathema "Εἴ τις λέγει ἢ ἔχει, πρόσκαιρον εἶναι τὴν τῶν δαιμόνων καὶ ἀσεβῶν ἀνθρώπων κόλασιν, καὶ τέλος κατά τινα χρόνον αὐτὴν ἕξειν, ἤγουν ἀποκατάστασιν γενέσθαι δαιμόνων ἢ ἀσεβῶν ἀνθρώπων, ἀνάθεμα ἔστω": "Ἀνάθεμα καὶ Ὠριγένει τῷ καὶ Ἀδαμαντίῳ τῷ ταῦτα ἐκθεμένῳ μετὰ τῶν μυσαρῶν αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐπικαταράτων ἐναγῶν τε δογμάτων, καὶ παντὶ προσώπῳ φρονοῦντι ταῦτα, ἢ ἐκδικοῦντι, ἢ [κατά τινα τρόπον] κατά τι παντελῶς ἐν οἱῳδήποτε χρόνῳ τούτων ἀντιποιεῖσθαι τολμῶντι."
Latin (PG 86.1, col. 990): "Anathema et Origeni, qui et Adamantius, qui haec exposuit una cum nefaria atque exsecrabili scelestaque ejus doctrina, et cuivis qui ea sentiat, aut defendat, aut ullo modo prorsus quocunque tempore tueri [last word illegible in the OCR]."

**English** (Own translation. Compare NPNF II.14 (public domain): "Anathema to Origen and to that Adamantius, who set forth these opinions together with his nefarious and execrable and wicked doctrine...")

> Ninth anathema (own translation): "If anyone says or holds that the punishment of demons and impious men is temporary and will have an end at some time, that is, that there will be a restoration (apokatastasis) of demons or of impious men: let him be anathema." Contested rendering: ἀποκατάστασις is 'restoration' here, not 'universal salvation'; the sentence speaks of demons and impious men and says nothing of salvation. Closing anathema: "Anathema also to Origen, who is also called Adamantius, who set out these things, together with his loathsome, accursed and abominable doctrines, and to every person who thinks these things or defends them, or in any way at all, at any time whatever, dares to take their part."

**Edition:** E. Schwartz, ACO III (Berlin 1940), pp. 189-214 (page range as cited in the earlier draft; I could not confirm it), the page and line of the closing anathema not confirmed. Older printings: Labbe-Cossart, Concilia V, col. 677 (cited by NPNF); Mansi IX; PG 86.1, cols. 945-990.  
**Checked against:** PG 86.1, cols. 989-990, in the Open Greek and Latin OCR (OGL-PatrologiaGraecaDev/Vol.-86.1, hvd.32044019208909, files 502.txt and 502.hocr), downloaded again and compared word for word. OCR errors corrected: Ὠριγὲνει, ’Δδαμαντίῳ, ἑκθεμίνῳ, ἑπικαταράτων, οίῳδῆποτε, τολμῶντε (read τολμῶντι), de— fendat; the Latin ends in OCR garbage after 'tueri'. ACO III was not available. ([source 1](https://github.com/OGL-PatrologiaGraecaDev/Vol.-86.1), [source 2](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.x.html), [source 3](https://archive.org/stream/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek_djvu.txt), [source 4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OGL-PatrologiaGraecaDev/Vol.-86.1/master/hvd.32044019208909_ocr/502.txt), [source 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OGL-PatrologiaGraecaDev/Vol.-86.1/master/hvd.32044019208909_ocr/502.hocr))

**About the text:** The NPNF editor says the text is 'corrupt, at all events the Latin and Greek do not agree'. In the text I checked, the two agree on the name: Greek τῷ καὶ Ἀδαμαντίῳ and Latin 'qui et Adamantius' both mean 'who is also called Adamantius', one person. NPNF's 'Origen and to that Adamantius' makes two people out of one and is a contested rendering; in my reading the Greek does not support it. NPNF cites Labbe and Cossart (col. 677) as its source, and I could not see that edition's Latin, so the editor's note ('the Latin and Greek do not agree') may describe a difference in that text that Migne's does not have. Where the versions differ is in the rest: Greek δόγματα (plural, 'doctrines') against Latin 'doctrina'; παντὶ προσώπῳ ('every person') against 'cuivis'; ἀντιποιεῖσθαι ('to lay claim to, take the side of') against 'tueri' ('protect'). The words in square brackets are an alternative reading printed by Migne. The Latin in PG is a translation printed with the Greek; I did not establish whether it is ancient or early modern, and I found no separate ancient Latin version. ACO III may differ in detail from PG.

**How scholars read it**

- **Henry R. Percival (NPNF editor)**, NPNF II.14 (1900), note to 'The Anathematisms of the Emperor Justinian against Origen'. Thinks the closing text is corrupt and that the Greek and Latin do not agree. He does not say which words he means. (*doubts the text, not the condemnation*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.x.html))
- **Aloisius Vincenzi (as reported by Diekamp)**, In S. Gregorii Nysseni et Origenis scripta et doctrinam nova recensio, as reported in Diekamp, Die origenistischen Streitigkeiten im sechsten Jahrhundert (1899), Einleitung p. 4. Went further than Halloix: declared even Justinian's edict against Origen spurious, 'a document handed to the emperor and produced in Syria', in Diekamp's German paraphrase ('nur ein dem Kaiser überreichtes, in Syrien entstandenes Aktenstück'). (*denies the edict is genuine*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://archive.org/stream/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek_djvu.txt))
- **Karl Joseph von Hefele (as reported by Diekamp)**, Conciliengeschichte (2nd ed.), as reported in Diekamp (1899), Einleitung p. 5. Held a middle view: the fifth council named Origen only 'nur transeundo und in cumulo' (in passing and in a group) in its eleventh anathema, so the real proceedings against him belong to the earlier synod of 543. (*the 543 edict is the real condemnation of Origen*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://archive.org/stream/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek_djvu.txt))
- **Richard Price**, The Acts of the Council of Constantinople of 553 (2009), as quoted by Kimel 2020. The 543 canons were issued as an imperial decree, so 'their authority was imperial rather than synodal'. (*accepts the edict; limits its authority*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2020/05/31/did-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-condemn-universal-salvation/))


### 3. 551: Justinian's confession of faith lists the same heretics without Origen

**Source:** Justinian, Confession of the Right Faith against the Three Chapters (Ὁμολογία πίστεως / Confessio rectae fidei), tenth anathema · **Date:** 551  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

This is the parallel that the case for interpolation rests on. Two years before the council, the emperor's own list of heretics stops at Eutyches, and the council's eleventh anathema has the same list with Origen added at the end.

**Greek, with the Latin printed beside it in Migne**

> Greek (PG 86.1, col. 1017): "Εἴ τις μὴ ἀναθεματίζει Ἄρειον, Εὐνόμιον, Μακεδόνιον, Ἀπολινάριον, Νεστόριον, Εὐτυχέα, καὶ τοὺς τὰ ὅμοια τούτοις φρονοῦντας, ἢ φρονήσαντας, ἀνάθεμα ἔστω."
Latin (PG 86.1, col. 1018): "Si quis non anathematizat Arium, Eunomium, Macedonium, Apollinarium, Nestorium, Eutychen, et eos qui similia eis sapuerunt vel sapiunt, talis anathema sit."

**English** (Own translation (standard English: K. P. Wesche, On the Person of Christ: The Christology of Emperor Justinian, 1991).)

> "If anyone does not anathematize Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius, Apollinarius, Nestorius, Eutyches, and those who think or have thought the like, let him be anathema."

**Edition:** E. Schwartz, Drei dogmatische Schriften Iustinians (Abhandlungen der Bayerischen Akademie der Wissenschaften, phil.-hist. Abt., NF 18, Munich 1939); page not confirmed. PG 86.1, cols. 993-1036 (the Confessio begins at col. 993).  
**Checked against:** PG 86.1, cols. 1017-1018, in the OGL OCR (hvd.32044019208909, file 516.txt), downloaded again: the Latin matches exactly; the Greek matches except at Macedonius, which the OCR gives as 'Μακεδόνων' (I print Μακεδόνιον on the strength of the Latin and canon 11; the page image was not seen). The second scan named in the earlier draft was not re-opened. Schwartz 1939 was not available. ([source 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OGL-PatrologiaGraecaDev/Vol.-86.1/master/hvd.32044019208909_ocr/516.txt), [source 2](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm), [source 3](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ilaria-ramelli-on-second-council-of-constantinople.pdf))

**About the text:** Both OCR scans read Μακεδόνων. I print Μακεδόνιον because the Latin has 'Macedonium' and the council's canon 11 has Μακεδόνιον; I did not see the page image. Hefele calls this 'the tenth in the omologia'. In the PG Latin (col. 1018) the Arius list is the last sentence of the section numbered X ('X. Anathematizat enim et eos qui per partem dividunt ... Si quis non anathematizat Arium ...'), and the next section is numbered XI, which agrees with Hefele's 'tenth'. Ramelli, Harding and Kimel call the document the 'Homonoia' (Ramelli glosses it 'Concord'). I could not identify a separate document of that name. The list they describe is the one printed here, in the Homologia ('Confession'), so 'Homonoia' looks like a slip. Canon 11 of 553 differs from this text in more than Origen's name: it adds 'with their impious writings', the clause about all other heretics condemned by the church and the four councils, and the words about those who 'persisted in their impiety until death'.

**How scholars read it**

- **Karl Joseph von Hefele**, A History of the Councils of the Church, Eng. trans., vol. iv, p. 336, as reproduced in NPNF II.14 notes to Capitulum XI. Grants that the eleventh anathema 'is identical with the tenth in the omologia of the Emperor, but in the latter the name of Origen is lacking'. But he finds this too weak to change the text: 'it is quite possible that either the Emperor subsequently went further than in his omologia, or that the bishops at the fifth Synod, of their own accord, added Origen'. (*reads the name in canon 11 as original*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm))
- **Halloix, Garnier, Basnage, Walch and Vincenzi (as reported by Hefele)**, Reported in Hefele, History of the Councils, vol. iv, p. 336 (NPNF II.14). ### 4. 553: Justinian's letter to the bishops names Origen; the fifteen anathemas do not

**Source:** Justinian, Letter to the holy synod concerning Origen and those like-minded with him (Epistula ad synodum de Origene), followed in the tradition by the fifteen anathemas against Origenist teaching · **Date:** 553, before the council opened on 5 May (Diekamp's dating); Migne's editor placed the letter at the synod under Menas c. 543  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

This answers the question whether Origen is named in the fifteen anathemas or only in a heading. He is in neither. He is named in the emperor's covering letter, which asks the bishops to anathematize each chapter 'together with the impious Origen'. The title 'Anathemas against Origen' is a modern editor's.

**Greek (letter, with Migne's Latin); the fifteen anathemas in Greek (not checked)**

> Heading (PG 86.1, col. 989): "Γράμμα τοῦ βασιλέως Ἰουστινιανοῦ πρὸς τὴν ἁγίαν σύνοδον περὶ Ὠριγένους καὶ τῶν ὁμοφρόνων αὐτοῦ."
Letter (col. 991): "Ἐπεὶ τοίνυν διέγνωσται ἡμῖν, ὥς τινες ἐν Ἱεροσολύμοις εἰσὶ μοναχοὶ δήπουθεν, Πυθαγόρᾳ, καὶ Πλάτωνι, καὶ Ὠριγένει τῷ Ἀδαμαντίῳ, καὶ τῇ τούτων δυσσεβείᾳ καὶ πλάνῃ κατακολουθοῦντες, καὶ διδάσκοντες ..."
(col. 993): "... προτρέπομεν τοὺς ὁσιωτάτους ὑμᾶς, εἰς ἓν συνηγμένους, ἐπιμελῶς ἐντυχεῖν τῇ ὑποτεταγμένῃ ἐκθέσει, καὶ ἕκαστον τῶν αὐτοῦ κεφαλαίων κατακρῖναί τε, καὶ ἀναθεματίσαι μετὰ τοῦ δυσσεβοῦς Ὠριγένους καὶ πάντων τῶν τὰ τοιαῦτα φρονούντων, ἢ φρονησόντων εἰς τέλος."
Latin (col. 990, editor's footnote to the heading): "Pertinent hae litterae ad synodum a Menna celebratam cir. an. Ch. 543."

**English** (Own translation of the letter; standard English in Price 2009, vol. 2, pp. 282-284 (as cited by Kimel 2026, part 4). The manuscript heading is quoted from NPNF II.14 (public domain).)

> Heading: "Letter of the emperor Justinian to the holy synod concerning Origen and those who think as he did." "Since, then, it has come to our knowledge that there are some at Jerusalem, monks no less, who follow Pythagoras and Plato and Origen Adamantius, and their impiety and error, and who teach them ..." "... we urge your holinesses, gathered into one, to read carefully the exposition set out below and to condemn and anathematize each of its chapters, together with the impious Origen and all who think such things, or will think them to the end."
The fifteen anathemas themselves name no one. In the Vienna manuscript they are headed "Canons of the 165 holy Fathers of the holy fifth Synod, held in Constantinople" (NPNF).

Fifteen anathemas: Straub, ACO IV.1, pp. 248-249 (page not confirmed); Mansi IX.  
**Checked against:** Letter: PG 86.1, cols. 989-993, in the OGL OCR (hvd.32044019208909, files 502-504), downloaded again and compared; heading, opening, the Jerusalem passage and the closing request match once OCR errors (Ὠριγίνει, ’Δδαμαντίῳ, δυσσεϐείκ, προτρέπεμεν, ἶκαστον, ὁμογρόνων) are corrected. **About the text:** Migne's editor attached the letter to the synod of c. 543, printing it straight after the 543 edict. Diekamp (1899) argued that the letter and the fifteen anathemas belong to spring 553, before the council opened, and most scholars now follow him. The phrase 'the exposition set out below' links the letter to a set of chapters; which set it is depends on that dating. The OCR reads φρονησόντων εἰς τέλος after φρονούντων, ἢ, and the Latin has 'aut sentient ad finem', so the phrase is printed and translated above (an earlier draft left it out as uncertain). δήπουθεν ('doubtless, of course') is ironical: 'monks, forsooth'. Hefele, Walch and Döllinger regarded the Vienna heading as untrustworthy (NPNF).

- **Franz Diekamp (as reported by Kimel)**, Die origenistischen Streitigkeiten im sechsten Jahrhundert (Münster, 1899), as described in Kimel 2026, part 3. (*anathemas and letter belong to 553, outside the council's sessions*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/))
- **Karl Joseph von Hefele**, History of the Councils, vol. iv, p. 221 sq., as quoted in NPNF II.14 excursus. Assigned the fifteen anathemas to the synod of 543 while admitting 'We certainly possess no strong and decisive proof'. He also distrusted the Vienna heading that gives them to the fifth synod. (*anathemas belong to 543*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))
- **David Bentley Hart**, 'Saint Origen', First Things, 1 October 2015. Writes that the fifteen anathemas 'were never even discussed by the assembled bishops' (and, he adds, were not approved or formally issued by the council), that few of them resemble Origen's actual ideas except in a distorted form, and that Origen 'in fact is never named in any of them'. (*denies the fifteen are an act of the council and says they do not match Origen's ideas*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://firstthings.com/saint-origen/))
- **Richard Price**, The Acts of the Council of Constantinople of 553 (Liverpool, 2009), as quoted by Kimel 2020 (n. 20); the volume and page of the phrase are not given there. Price prints Justinian's letter to the bishops in vol. 2, pp. 282-284 (Kimel 2026, part 4, n. 8). (*Origenism occupied the bishops alongside the council's business (as quoted)*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2020/05/31/did-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-condemn-universal-salvation/))

- **Henry R. Percival (editor of NPNF II.14)**, NPNF II.14 (1900), excursus on the XV anathemas. Reaches no firm verdict on whether the fifth council adopted the fifteen anathemas: a dogmatic answer would be 'the height of rashness' on the present evidence. He leans against direct adoption ('nothing whatever is said about Origen in the call of the council'), but holds that, even if they were first adopted at the home synod, putting Origen's name in the eleventh canon 'practically ratified and made its own the action of that Synod'. (*undecided; canon 11 would then adopt the home synod's act*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))

(*the fifteen were added to the Greek Acts later*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/))


### 5. 553, fifth session: Origen named as already anathematized 'even now'

**Source:** Council of Constantinople II, Acts, session V (17 May 553), the discussion of whether heretics may be condemned after death (Price's paragraph 87); speaker not named on the tertullian.org copy of NPNF (see textual_notes) · **Date:** 17 May 553  
**How far it was checked:** R, not yet checked against the text.

This is the second place where the Acts of 553 themselves name Origen. If genuine, it shows the council in session treating him as already condemned, by the bishops present and by Vigilius. That is why defenders of interpolation have had to reject it as well as canon 11.

**Latin (the Greek Acts are lost)**

> Not obtained. I could not open the Latin of this passage (Mansi IX, col. 272 D; Straub, ACO IV.1, session V). No scholar I read quotes it in Latin. The English below is the NPNF's.

**English** (NPNF II.14 (1900), excursus on the XV anathemas, public domain. Price 2009 renders the last clause with 'even now' and 'your holinesses' in the plural (as quoted by Kimel 2020, n. 29); NPNF has 'your holiness' in the singular.)

> "And we found that many others had been anathematised after death, also even Origen; and if any one were to go back to the times of Theophilus of blessed memory or further, he would have found him anathematised after death; which also now your holiness and Vigilius, the most religious Pope of Old Rome has done in his case."

**Edition:** J. Straub, ACO IV.1 (Berlin 1971), session V, page not confirmed; Mansi IX, col. 272 D (given in the earlier draft as Diekamp's reference; I could not confirm it); Price 2009, vol. 1, session V, para. 87 (as cited by Truglia).  
**Checked against:** English only: NPNF II.14 excursus on the XV anathemas (public domain), re-read on tertullian.org, where the sentence matches word for word; and Price's translation as quoted by Truglia 2019 and Kimel 2020. ([source 1](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm), [source 2](https://orthodoxchristiantheology.com/2019/11/17/apocatastasis-condemnation-during-the-council-of-constantinople-ii/), [source 3](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2020/05/31/did-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-condemn-universal-salvation/), [source 4](https://archive.org/stream/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek_djvu.txt))

**About the text:** The speaker is not settled. The tertullian.org copy of the NPNF excursus (read again 2026-10-20) introduces the words only as 'these words now found in the acts as we have them' and names no speaker. The scholarship pass reported that CCEL's copy of the same page gives the speech to 'Ascidas'; CCEL is blocked from this environment, so the two copies could not be compared, and the attribution is not used here. Price doubts that Ascidas was an Origenist at all (vol. 1, p. 19 n. 40, pointing to vol. 2, pp. 273-4), which undercuts the old argument that Ascidas would have protected Origen. The two English wordings differ in tense and subject (NPNF: 'which also now your holiness and Vigilius ... has done in his case'; Truglia: 'This has been done even now in his regard by your holinesses and by Vigilius'). 'Even now' can be read as the 543 edict or as a synod held just before the council; nothing in the passage settles which. Diekamp reports the objection that 'your holiness' and Vigilius may refer only to their assent to Justinian's 543 edict (Vigilius did subscribe that edict, according to Price as reported by Truglia). Halloix treated this passage and canon 11 as 'unzweifelhafte Fälschungen' (undoubted forgeries), and Vincenzi declared it tampered with. The surrounding argument, that heretics may be condemned after death, is the one the council needed for Theodore of Mopsuestia, who died in 428. The Latin (ACO IV.1, session V) and the Mansi column were not found by anyone in this survey.

Diekamp calls the book 'eine krasse Tendenzschrift' (a crassly partisan work). (*reads it as interpolated*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://archive.org/stream/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek_djvu.txt))

### 6. 553, eighth session, canon 11: Origen last in the list of heretics

**Source:** Council of Constantinople II, session VIII (2 June 553), anathemas (capitula) against the Three Chapters, canon 11 · **Date:** 2 June 553  
**How far it was checked:** S, checked against a scholar's quotation of it.

This is the only place where an anathema of the council itself names Origen. The names, in order, are Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius, Apollinarius, Nestorius, Eutyches, Origen. Every argument that Origen's name was added later is an argument about this line. Every argument that the name is a later addition is an argument about this line. Two things should be kept apart: whether the name is in the text of canon 11 (every reported witness has it) and whether it was in the text the bishops approved (the evidence is inferential either way).

**Latin (Acts); Greek (text of the canons preserved outside the Latin Acts)**

> Latin: "Si quis non anathematizat Arium, Eunomium, Macedonium, Apollinarium, Nestorium, Eutychen, Origenem cum impiis eorum conscriptis, et alios omnes haereticos, qui condemnati et anathematizati sunt a sancta catholica et apostolica Ecclesia et a praedictis sanctis quattuor Conciliis, et eos, qui similia praedictis haereticis sapuerunt vel sapiunt et usque ad mortem in sua impietate permanserunt vel permanent, talis anathema sit."
Greek: "Εἴ τις μὴ ἀναθεματίζει Ἄρειον, Εὐνόμιον, Μακεδόνιον, Ἀπολινάριον, Νεστόριον, Εὐτυχέα, καὶ Ὠριγένην, μετὰ τῶν ἀσεβῶν αὐτῶν συγγραμμάτων, καὶ τοὺς ἄλλους πάντας αἱρετικούς, τοὺς κατακριθέντας καὶ ἀναθεματισθέντας ὑπὸ τῆς ἁγίας καθολικῆς καὶ ἀποστολικῆς Ἐκκλησίας καὶ τῶν προειρημένων ἁγίων τεσσάρων συνόδων, καὶ τοὺς τὰ ὅμοια τῶν προειρημένων αἱρετικῶν φρονήσαντας ἢ φρονοῦντας καὶ μέχρι τέλους τῇ οἰκείᾳ ἀσεβείᾳ ἐμμείναντας, ὁ τοιοῦτος ἀνάθεμα ἔστω."

**English** (NPNF II.14 (1900), 'The Capitula of the Council', public domain. Price 2009 and Tanner 1990 are the standard modern translations.)

> "If anyone does not anathematize Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius, Apollinaris, Nestorius, Eutyches and Origen, as well as their impious writings, as also all other heretics already condemned and anathematized by the Holy Catholic and Apostolic Church, and by the aforesaid four Holy Synods and [if anyone does not equally anathematize] all those who have held and hold or who in their impiety persist in holding to the end the same opinion as those heretics just mentioned: let him be anathema."

**Edition:** J. Straub, ACO IV.1 (Berlin 1971): Latin at p. 218, lines 8-9; Greek at p. 242, lines 32-33 (both references as given by Livorsi 2025, n. 38). Mansi IX, col. 384 AB (Latin; reference from Diekamp). Denzinger-Hünermann 433 (older numbering 223). Also printed in Tanner (ed.), Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils I (1990), which keeps Origen in canon 11 (Tanner leaves out the fifteen anathemas, not this canon). Garnier's Dissertatio de V. Synodo (Paris 1675; PL 68, col. 1074B) quotes the canon from an older printing.  
**Checked against:** Latin: Denzinger-Hünermann 433 on clerus.org, re-opened and matching word for word ('talis an. s.' expanded). The Origen clause in both languages is confirmed by L. Livorsi, Greek, Roman, and Byzantine Studies 65 (2025), n. 38, quoting ACO IV.1 (Latin 218.8-9, Greek 242.32-33). The rest of the Greek is from an unsourced Greek blog (ostratiotistisagapis.blogspot.com, 2014), identical to the draft; only its opening and Origen clause are independently confirmed. Second Latin witness for the same clause: Jean Garnier's 1675 quotation (PL 68.1074B, Latin Wikisource transcription), which has the name in the same place. The Origen clause in both languages was read again on 2026-10-20 in Livorsi's footnote 38 (GRBS 65, p. 64). I did not see Straub's apparatus. ([source 1](http://ostratiotistisagapis.blogspot.com/2014/11/blog-post_62.html), [source 2](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ilaria-ramelli-on-second-council-of-constantinople.pdf), [source 3](https://grbs.library.duke.edu/index.php/grbs/article/download/17027/7595/23050), [source 4](https://la.m.wikisource.org/wiki/Dissertatio_ad_Liberatum), [source 5](https://repozytorium.uwb.edu.pl/jspui/bitstream/11320/19265/1/Elpis_27_2025_W_Slomski_Theological_Motives_for_Condemning_Origenism.pdf), [source 6](https://shamelessorthodoxy.com/2021/01/21/orthodoxy-universal-salvation-are-the-two-compatible/), [source 7](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-110.htm), [source 8](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm), [source 9](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.vii.html), [source 10](https://www.clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/en/dvg.htm), [source 11](https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/ecum05.htm))

**About the text:** The Greek original of the Acts is lost (Ramelli). The Latin Acts are a translation; Hefele thought the Roman copy of them was probably prepared for Vigilius himself (NPNF II.14). Straub's Latin spells 'Arrium' (Livorsi). The Greek has 'καὶ Ὠριγένην' where the Latin in Denzinger runs 'Eutychen, Origenem' without 'et'. The Greek of the canons survives outside the Latin Acts. Bidez and Parmentier's notes to Evagrius point to George the Monk for the Greek of the council's sentence, but I did not confirm which witness Straub used for canon 11. No scholar I read reports a manuscript of the Latin or the Greek that lacks Origen's name; the case for interpolation is inferential. Compared with the 551 Homologia, the canon adds not only Origen but also 'with their impious writings', the four councils, and 'persisted in their impiety until death'. That last clause matches the council's argument in session V for condemning the dead. Garnier's 1675 quotation reads 'Apollinarem' and 'cum impiis eorum scriptis' where Denzinger and Straub (as Livorsi quotes him) read 'Apollinarium' and 'conscriptis'; these are differences of form elsewhere in the clause, and none touches the name. Tanner's English has 'their heretical books' for the same words. State of the Acts, from sources opened on 2026-10-20: Livorsi says the Acts 'are solely available in a Latin translation' except for a few documents (p. 59), made 'in the immediate aftermath of the council' (p. 60), and lists the canons among the few parts that survive in Greek (p. 63, nn. 35-37); he calls the Latin 'extremely literal' (p. 64, n. 38). He names no manuscript of the Constantinople II Acts (the sigla he discusses belong to the Acts of Ephesus) and is not writing about interpolation. The NPNF excursus on the Acts says that 'between the Roman Manuscript printed by Labbe and the Paris manuscript found in Mansi there are considerable variations', and that some of the most damaging material about Vigilius appears only in the Paris manuscript; nobody I read says whether the two differ at canon 11. Ohme (2022, abstract) reports a longer first edition on papyrus rolls and a shorter second edition in codices. Price, as Kimel reports him, says the Latin translation 'was likely produced soon after the finalization of the Greek text'. Hefele's page prints 'the monks of the new Lama in Palestine', evidently a misprint for Lavra. Gregory the Great's summary of the four councils (see other_findings) gives Dioscorus with Eutyches and does not name Apollinaris; canon 11 names Apollinaris and not Dioscorus.

**How scholars read it**

- **Karl Joseph von Hefele**, A History of the Councils of the Church, Eng. trans., vol. iv, p. 336 (as reproduced in NPNF II.14). Keeps the name. He sets out four reasons given by those who hold it inserted: (1) Theodore Ascidas, 'the Origenist', was one of the most influential members of the Synod; (2) 'only such heretics would be named as had been condemned by one of the first four Ecumenical Synods'; (3) the anathema 'is identical with the tenth in the omologia of the Emperor', where Origen's name is lacking; (4) Origen's errors differ from those of the group. He judges these 'of insufficient strength, or mere conjecture', and allows that 'either the Emperor subsequently went further than in his omologia, or that the bishops at the fifth Synod, of their own accord, added Origen'. The Roman copy of the Acts, 'which has the highest credibility, and was probably prepared for Vigilius himself', contains the name. He also points to the Origenist monks of the New Lavra in Palestine, who broke communion with bishops who signed the Acts, and to 'the widely-circulated ancient rumour that our Synod anathematized Origen and the Origenists', which he thinks is explained only if the name stood in canon 11. (Diekamp reports Hefele's view that the council named him only 'nur transeundo und in cumulo', in passing and in a group.) Note that on Hefele's own second alternative the name is an addition to the emperor's text, made by the bishops at the council. (*name original*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm))

### 7. 553-554: Pope Vigilius's letter to Eutychius and his second Constitutum

**Source:** Vigilius, Letter to Eutychius of Constantinople ('Scandala', 8 December 553); Constitutum II pro damnatione trium capitulorum (23 February 554) · **Date:** 8 December 553; 23 February 554  
**How far it was checked:** R, not yet checked against the text.

Interpolation theorists point out that the pope's own confirmations of the council never name Origen. The text matters because it shows what kind of silence this is.

**Latin and Greek (the letter); Latin (Constitutum II)**

> Not obtained. I could not open the Latin or Greek of either document. The English below of the letter's anathema comes from the NPNF translation, which follows the text in Labbe-Cossart and PL 69.

**English** (NPNF II.14 (1900), public domain; Price 2009, vol. 2, for the standard modern translation.)

> Letter to Eutychius: "we condemn and anathematize Theodore, formerly bishop of Mopsuestia, and his impious writings, together with all other heretics, who (as is manifest) have been condemned and anathematized by the four holy Synods aforesaid, and by the Catholic Church: also the writings of Theodoret which are opposed to the right faith ... Moreover we anathematize and condemn the letter to the Persian heretic Maris, which is said to have been written by Ibas ..."

**Edition:** Letter to Eutychius: Straub, ACO IV.1 (1971), page not confirmed; PL 69, col. 121 ff.; Labbe-Cossart V, col. 596 ff. (both as cited by NPNF). Constitutum II: E. Schwartz, ACO IV.2 (1914), commonly cited as pp. 138-168 (page not confirmed); PL 69, col. 143 ff. (not confirmed).

([source 1](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.xii.html), [source 2](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ilaria-ramelli-on-second-council-of-constantinople.pdf), [source 3](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/12/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-4/), [source 4](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/11306b.htm))

(Nestorius and Apollinaris do appear in the letter, but only in its descriptions of what Theodoret's and Ibas's writings defended or said, not in an anathema; the letter is dated 'VI. Id. Dec.', 8 December 553.) Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius and Eutyches are absent, just as Origen is. So the letter has no list parallel to canon 11 from which Origen could be missing. I could not check whether the Constitutum II has such a list. NPNF reports old doubts about the letter's genuineness, which most scholars reject.

She treats this as part of the case that the name in canon 11 is an interpolation. Origen's name is absent from the earliest draft of the anathemas and 'does not appear in the version of the conciliar proceedings that was eventually signed by Pope Vigillius [sic in Wikipedia], a long time afterwards'. This 'may indicate that Origen's name may have been retrospectively inserted into the text after the Council'. (*silence supports interpolation*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origenist_crises))
- **Fr Aidan Kimel**, 'Apokatastasis, Origen, and the Fifth Ecumenical Council (Part 4)', Eclectic Orthodoxy, 12 Jan 2026. In his summary of Ramelli's argument he writes: 'Even more strikingly, the official documents of Pope Vigilius, eventually promulgated in connection with the council, do not contain Origen's name at all.' The sentence is his own, and he draws no separate conclusion about Vigilius from it; he goes on to say that, if Ramelli is right, canon 11 'cannot function as a precise doctrinal judgment against universal salvation'. (*reports the silence as part of Ramelli's case*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/12/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-4/))

### 8. Evagrius Scholasticus (c. 593): the council's acclamations against Origen

**Source:** Evagrius Scholasticus, Ecclesiastical History 4.38 · **Date:** c. 593-594  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

This is the main ancient witness that the bishops of 553 condemned Origen by name. It was the chief evidence for Noris and the Ballerini, and the NPNF editor thought it was set aside too quickly.

**Greek**

> "Καὶ μετὰ ἔνια, ἐξέθεντο τέσσαρα καὶ δέκα κεφάλαια περὶ τῆς ὀρθῆς καὶ ἀμωμήτου πίστεως. Καὶ ταῦτα μὲν οὕτω προκεχωρήκει. Ἐκ λιβέλλων δὲ ἐπιδεδομένων πρὸς Εὐλογίου, Κόνωνος, Κυριακοῦ, καὶ Παγκρατίου μοναχῶν κατὰ τῶν Ὠριγένους τοῦ καὶ Ἀδαμαντίου δογμάτων καὶ τῶν ἑπομένων τῇ τούτου δυσσεβείᾳ καὶ πλάνῃ, πυνθάνεται Ἰουστινιανὸς τῆς ἁλισθείσης συνόδου περὶ τούτων ... Ἀναφορὰ τοίνυν γέγονε πρὸς Ἰουστινιανὸν παρὰ τῆς συνόδου μετὰ τὰς γενομένας παρ’ αὐτῆς ἐκβοήσεις κατὰ Ὠριγένους καὶ τῶν τὰ αὐτὰ ἐκείνῳ πεπλανημένων. ... Ἀνηνέχθησαν αὐτοῖς καὶ Διδύμου καὶ Εὐαγρίου καὶ Θεοδώρου ἕτεραι πολλαὶ βλασφημίαι μάλα σπουδαίως τὰ περὶ τούτων ἐκλεξαμένοις."

**English** (Own translation; compare E. Walford (1846, public domain) and M. Whitby, The Ecclesiastical History of Evagrius Scholasticus (Liverpool 2000), pp. 248-259.)

> "And after some other things they set out fourteen chapters on the right and blameless faith. So these matters went forward. But on the strength of petitions submitted by the monks Eulogius, Conon, Cyriacus and Pancratius against the doctrines of Origen, also called Adamantius, and of those who follow his impiety and error, Justinian questioned the assembled synod about these things ... A report was therefore made to Justinian by the synod, after the acclamations it had made against Origen and those who had strayed into the same errors as he. ... Many other blasphemies of Didymus, Evagrius and Theodore were also reported to them, who had collected the material about these men with great zeal."

**Edition:** J. Bidez and L. Parmentier, The Ecclesiastical History of Evagrius with the Scholia (London 1898), pp. 188-189; PG 86.2, cols. 2780-2784 (as cited by Misiarczyk).  
**Checked against:** First1KGreek digital edition of Bidez-Parmentier (tlg2733.tlg001.1st1K-grc1.xml; OCR corrected by Annette von Stockhausen), downloaded again: every Greek word of the quotation matches, and the passage runs over pp. 188-189. ([source 1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGreekAndLatin/First1KGreek/master/data/tlg2733/tlg001/tlg2733.tlg001.1st1K-grc1.xml), [source 2](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm), [source 3](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/))

**About the text:** Evagrius quotes the council's sentence against the Three Chapters (with its 'we condemn and anathematize ... all other heretics') without naming Origen, and says only that the council 'set out fourteen chapters'. He does not quote canon 11. He puts the Origen business after those chapters ('So these matters went forward'), as a separate proceeding begun by the monks' petitions, and he quotes the synod's address to Justinian ('Τῆς ἄνωθεν εὐγενείας ... ἐφύγομεν ταύτην'). The 'fifth chapter' he cites is called, in his Greek, 'the fifth chapter of the blasphemies of private persons of the so-called New Lavra' (πέμπτον κεφάλαιον τῶν βλασφημηθέντων ἀπὸ ἰδικῶν προσώπων τῆς καλουμένης νέας λαύρας), and holds Theodore Askidas's saying about the apostles in the restoration; Hefele (via Oxenham, quoted in Kimel 2026, part 3, n. 14) says this proposition is not among the fifteen anathemas. The 'fourteen chapters' that Evagrius says the council set out agree in number with the fourteen canons of the council as usually printed (canon 11 is one of them), not with the fifteen anti-Origenist anathemas; he does not say whether Origen is named in them. Which Theodore is meant in 'Didymus, Evagrius and Theodore' is not stated. ἐκβοήσεις are acclamations: shouted declarations, the normal way a synod recorded assent.

**How scholars read it**

- **Henry R. Percival (NPNF editor)**, NPNF II.14 (1900), excursus on the XV anathemas. 'Evagrius, the historian, distinctly says that Origen was condemned with special anathemas at this Council, but his evidence is likewise (and, as it seems to me, too peremptorily) set aside.' (*takes Evagrius as evidence of conciliar condemnation*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))
- **Cardinal Henry Noris (as reported by NPNF)**, Dissertatio historica de synodo quinta (17th century; place and year not confirmed); reported in NPNF II.14. Held that the fifth synod condemned Origen twice: once by himself before the eight sessions whose Acts survive, and again after them together with his followers Didymus and Evagrius. (*the council condemned Origen*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))
- **Peter and Girolamo Ballerini (as reported by NPNF)**, Defensio of Noris's dissertation against Garnier; reported in NPNF II.14. Defended Noris's view against Garnier and, in NPNF's words, expanded and amended it. (*the council condemned Origen*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))
- **Michael Whitby (as quoted by Kimel)**, The Ecclesiastical History of Evagrius Scholasticus (Liverpool, 2000), p. 248 n. 131, as quoted in Kimel 2026, part 3. On the anti-Origenist proceedings Evagrius describes: 'they preceded the Council and were not regarded as a formal part of proceedings'. (*Evagrius describes proceedings outside the council's formal acts*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/))
- **Daniël Hombergen (as reported by Kimel)**, The Second Origenist Controversy (2001), p. 304 n. 236, as cited in Kimel 2026, part 3, n. 17. Says Evagrius relied on documents and on 'existing contradictory traditions' about what happened at the council. (*Evagrius's account rests on conflicting traditions*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/))


### 9. Cyril of Scythopolis (c. 557): 'a common and universal anathema' against Origen

**Source:** Cyril of Scythopolis, Life of Sabas 90 · **Date:** c. 556-558 (the date of composition is not confirmed)  
**How far it was checked:** S, checked against a scholar's quotation of it.

This is the nearest contemporary narrative. Cyril was a Palestinian monk (one of the orthodox monks sent to the New Lavra in 555, then at the Great Lavra of Saint Sabas, where he died c. 557-558) writing within a few years of the council, and he says a 'common and universal anathema' was directed against Origen and Theodore of Mopsuestia.

**Greek**

> Only a fragment checked: "... καὶ τὰ περὶ προϋπάρξεως καὶ ἀποκαταστάσεως Εὐαγρίῳ καὶ Διδύμῳ εἰρημένα ..." (the rest of the sentence, naming Origen and Theodore of Mopsuestia, was not obtained in Greek)

**English** (Own translation of the fragment; paraphrase of R. M. Price (trans.), Cyril of Scythopolis, The Lives of the Monks of Palestine (Cistercian 1991), p. 208, as quoted by Kimel 2026 part 3 and by Truglia 2019 (citing Price 2009, vol. 2, p. 270).)

> Fragment: "... and the things said by Evagrius and Didymus about pre-existence and restoration ..." In R. M. Price's translation the whole sentence says that when the fifth council met at Constantinople 'a common and universal anathema' was directed against Origen and Theodore of Mopsuestia and against this teaching of Evagrius and Didymus, in the presence and with the approval of the four patriarchs.

**Edition:** E. Schwartz, Kyrillos von Skythopolis (Texte und Untersuchungen 49.2, Leipzig 1939), p. 199 (as cited by Misiarczyk, who writes 'TU 9/2'); line not confirmed.  
**Checked against:** Quoted by L. Misiarczyk, Vox Patrum 65 (2016), n. 37, in a Greek font that the PDF stores in Symbol encoding ('kaˆ t¦ perˆ proup£rxewj kaˆ ¢pokatast£sewj EÙagr…J kaˆ DidÚmJ e„rhmšna'); I transcribed it into Greek letters. The English sense of the whole sentence is taken from Price's translation as quoted by Truglia and Kimel. ([source 1](https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/vp/article/download/3510/3470), [source 2](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-3/), [source 3](https://orthodoxchristiantheology.com/2019/11/17/apocatastasis-condemnation-during-the-council-of-constantinople-ii/))

**About the text:** ἀποκατάστασις is rendered 'restoration' here. Price's 'universal restoration' adds a word that is not in the Greek fragment, a contested rendering. Cyril joins in one sentence Origen, Theodore of Mopsuestia (condemned in the council's sentence and canons) and the teaching of Evagrius and Didymus (named in no surviving act of 553). Misiarczyk notes that Evagrius's name is absent from the council's Acts, from Justinian's letter to the synod and from the fifteen anathemas (and, as Hefele suggests, from the 543 synod's letter to Menas).

**How scholars read it**

### 10. 649, 680-81, 787: later councils name Origen with Didymus and Evagrius

**Source:** Lateran Synod (649), canon 18; Constantinople III (680-81), definition of faith (session XVIII); Nicaea II (787), definition of faith · **Date:** 649; 16 September 681; 13 October 787  
**How far it was checked:** S, checked against a scholar's quotation of it.

By 649 the West, by 681 an ecumenical council, and by 787 the last council the Orthodox and Catholic churches both count all describe the fifth council as directed against Origen, Didymus and Evagrius. In these texts the name is secure. The question is only what they are evidence of.

**Latin (649); Greek (681, 787)**

> Lateran 649, can. 18 (DH 518-520): "... id est, Sabellium, Arium, Eunomium, Macedonium, Apollinarem, Polemonem, Eutychen, Dioscurum, Timotheum Aelurum, Severum, Theodosium, Colluthum, Themistium, Paulum Samosatenum, Diodorum, Theodorum, Nestorium, Theodulum Persam, Origenem, Didymum, Evagrium, et compendiose omnes reliquos haereticos ..."
Constantinople III, definition: "πρὸς ταύταις καὶ τῇ τελευταίᾳ τούτων πέμπτῃ ἁγίᾳ συνόδῳ τῇ ἐνταῦθα συναθροισθείσῃ κατὰ Θεοδώρου τοῦ Μομψουεστίας, Ὠριγένους, Διδύμου τε καὶ Εὐαγρίου καὶ τῶν συγγραμμάτων Θεοδωρίτου ..."
Nicaea II, definition: "... καὶ τὰ Ὠριγένους, Εὐαγρίου τε καὶ Διδύμου μυθεύματα ἀναθεματίζομεν ..."

**English** (Own translation (649 and 681); NPNF II.14 (public domain) for 787.)

### 11. 680-81: Constantinople III finds forged documents in the Acts of 553

**Source:** Council of Constantinople III, Acts, sessions III, XII and XIV (examination of the codices of the fifth council's Acts) · **Date:** 680-681 (sessions III, XII and XIV; exact dates not confirmed)  
**How far it was checked:** R, not yet checked against the text.

This is what Ramelli's remark rests on, that 'suspicions have been raised already in 680 CE ... that the original Greek acts of the 553 CE council were interpolated'. It shows the Acts could be and were tampered with. It also shows what was tampered with, and that was not Origen.

**Greek (with a Latin version)**

> Not obtained. I could not open the Greek or Latin of sessions III, XII or XIV. The English below is Hefele's account as reproduced in NPNF.

**English** (NPNF II.14 (1900), excursus drawing on Hefele, public domain.)

> "At the XIVth Session of that synod the examination of the genuineness of the acts of the Second Council of Constantinople was resumed. It had been begun at the XIIth Session. Up to this time only two mss. had been used, now the librarian of the patriarchate presented a third ms. ... These were then compared and it was found that the two first agreed in containing the pretended letter of Mennas to Pope Vigilius, and the two writings addressed by Vigilius to Justinian and Theodora; but that none of these were found in the third ms. It was further found that the documents in dispute were in a different hand from the rest of the ms. and that in the first book of the parchment ms., three quarternions had been inserted, and in the second book between quarternions 15 and 16, four unpaged leaves had been placed." The council decreed that "these additions must be quashed in both mss., and marked with an obelus, and the falsifiers must be smitten with anathema", and acclaimed: "Anathema to the pretended letters of Mennas and Vigilius! Anathema to the forger of Acts!"

**Edition:** R. Riedinger, ACO ser. II vol. 2.1-2.2 (1990-1992), sessions III, XII and XIV (pages not confirmed); Mansi XI.  
**Checked against:** English only: NPNF II.14, 'Excursus on the Genuineness of the Acts of the Fifth Council' (public domain, drawing on Hefele), and a summary based on Hefele vol. 5 by S. Nesch on A. Psarev's Substack. ([source 1](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-110.htm), [source 2](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ilaria-ramelli-on-second-council-of-constantinople.pdf), [source 3](https://andreipsarev.substack.com/p/the-historical-methodology-of-the))

**About the text:** The inserted documents were a letter of Patriarch Menas to Vigilius and two letters of Vigilius to Justinian and Theodora teaching one will or one operation in Christ. They were evidence in the Monothelete dispute (the 7th-century quarrel over whether Christ had one will or two); the NPNF excursus as I re-read it does not say what the letters taught, and that they favoured one will is the usual account of the inquiry, not something I checked here. The papal legates are said to have challenged the Acts in session III. Nothing in the reports of the inquiry concerns Origen, canon 11 or the fifth session. The 680-81 council confirmed the rest of the codices it examined. In the same council's definition (passage 'later-councils-649-680-787') it names Origen among those the fifth council condemned. Ramelli's sentence, as published, is about the Greek Acts in general and does not say that the 680 suspicion touched Origen's name.

She does not say what the suspicions were about. (*tampering in 680 is a reason for caution about the Acts*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://afkimel.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ilaria-ramelli-on-second-council-of-constantinople.pdf))

Attacked the genuineness of the Acts at large, 'in defence of Pope Vigilius and of Origen'. (*the Acts as a whole are unreliable*; from a reliable report of the scholar's view; [source](https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-110.htm))


### 12. Halloix (1648): 'either the canon is corrupt, or the synod sinned'

**Source:** Pierre Halloix SJ, Origenes defensus (Liège, 1648), as reported by Jean Garnier SJ, Dissertatio de V. Synodo, cap. V §1; with Diekamp's description · **Date:** 1648 (Halloix); 1675 (Garnier); 1899 (Diekamp)  
**How far it was checked:** S, checked against a scholar's quotation of it.

This is where the interpolation thesis begins, and its two oldest arguments are already here in 1648. One is that the canon lists heretics condemned by the four earlier councils, which never condemned Origen. The other is that someone had a motive to tamper with the list. Halloix's guess about Dioscorus, the Alexandrian bishop deposed at Chalcedon in 451, would make the original list end in date order. The first argument is the one the interpolation side now rests on most heavily.

**Latin (Garnier reporting Halloix); German (Diekamp)**

> [Garnier, reporting Halloix:] Aut igitur canon ille adulterinus est, qua parte Origenis nomen intrusum est, aut a synodo graviter peccatum: quis enim secundum Ecclesiae regulas indicta causa condemnetur? [col. 1075C] Deinde nemo non intelligit ex verbis canonis in ipso tantum censeri haereticos qui praedictis quatuor in conciliis damnati sunt, velut Arius in primo, in secundo Eunomius, Macedonius et Apollinaris, Nestorius in tertio, in quarto Eutyches et Dioscorus. Cum igitur in nullo ex quatuor conciliis damnatus sit Origenes, videri potest ab Acephalo aliquo pro Dioscoro suppositus: quem tamen Acephalum stolide ac imprudenter fecisse fraudem oportuit, quam nempe ipsa produnt canonis verba, quae de solis haereticis antea damnatis ab Ecclesia in conciliis oecumenicis intelligi s[…]
[Diekamp 1899 on Halloix:] Bei seinem Streben, Origenes auch von dem leisesten Verdachte der Heterodoxie zu reinigen, leugnete er jede Verurtheilung seines Clienten auf der fünften allgemeinen Synode. […] Die beiden oben erwähnten Stellen in der fünften und achten Sitzung galten ihm als unzweifelhafte Fälschungen. […] Halloix' Buch war eine krasse Tendenzschrift.

**English** (Own translation of both. The Latin of the new sentence was read on 2026-10-20 in the Latin Wikisource transcription of PL 68.)

> [Garnier, reporting Halloix:] So either that canon is corrupt in the part where Origen's name has been thrust in, or the synod sinned gravely: for who, by the Church's rules, may be condemned without a hearing? [col. 1075C] Next, everyone sees that by the words of the canon only those heretics are counted in it who were condemned in the four councils named: Arius, for example, in the first; in the second Eunomius, Macedonius and Apollinaris; Nestorius in the third; in the fourth Eutyches and Dioscorus. Since, then, Origen was condemned in none of the four councils, it may seem that some Acephalus put him in place of Dioscorus. But that Acephalus must have done the fraud stupidly and carelessly, for the canon's own words betray it, since they can only be understood of heretics already condemned by the Church in ecumenical councils […]
[Diekamp:] In his effort to clear Origen of even the slightest suspicion of heterodoxy, he denied any condemnation of his client at the fifth general synod. […] The two passages mentioned above, in the fifth and eighth sessions, he regarded as undoubted forgeries. […] Halloix's book was a crass piece of special pleading.

**Edition:** P. Halloix, Origenes defensus sive Origenis Adamantii presbyteri, amatoris Iesu, vita, virtutes, documenta (Liège, 1648), page not confirmed. J. Garnier, Dissertatio de V. Synodo, appended to his edition of Liberatus's Breviarium (Paris, 1675); repr. PL 68, cols 1074C–1075C. F. Diekamp, Die origenistischen Streitigkeiten im sechsten Jahrhundert und das fünfte allgemeine Concil (Münster, 1899), Einleitung, pp. 2–3 (page approximate). Study: C. Falla, L'apologie d'Origène par Pierre Halloix (1648) (Paris: Les Belles Lettres, 1983; Bibl. Fac. Phil. et Lettres Liège 238).  
**Checked against:** Garnier: Latin Wikisource transcription of PL 68 (the transcription breaks off mid-word at 'intelligi s'). Diekamp: archive.org scan of the 1899 printing (OCR). Halloix's own book and Falla's study were not opened; Falla's open-access PDF did not render. ([source 1](https://la.m.wikisource.org/wiki/Dissertatio_ad_Liberatum), [source 2](https://dn790008.ca.archive.org/0/items/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek.pdf), [source 3](https://donum.uliege.be/handle/2268.1/14216))

**About the text:** Halloix's own Latin was not reached; the Latin given is Garnier's report of his reasoning in §I ('Quid Halloixius nuper de quaestione senserit'), so it may paraphrase him. The transcription breaks off at 'intelligi s[…]'. 'Acephali' ('the headless') was a name for the anti-Chalcedonian party, who would have had no reason to leave Dioscorus in a list of heretics. ### 13. The early defenders of the condemnation: Baronius, Huet, Noris, the Ballerini

**Source:** Franz Diekamp, Die origenistischen Streitigkeiten im sechsten Jahrhundert und das fünfte allgemeine Concil (1899), Einleitung · **Date:** 1899 (Diekamp), describing writers of 1588–1732  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

It shows the opposing camp, which held that the council itself condemned Origen. Its main argument was what ancient readers said happened, not the state of the Acts.

**German**

> Cäsar Baronius hielt dem Zustande der Synodalacten gegenüber das Zeugniß der alten Tradition für vollkommen ausreichend, um mit Sicherheit erweisen zu können, daß die fünfte allgemeine Synode Origenes und seine Lehren auf Grund besonderer Berathungen verurtheilt habe. Ihm schlössen sich, um nur einige hervorragende Vertreter dieser Ansicht zu nennen, mehr oder minder rückhaltlos Severinus Binius, Peter de Marca, Peter Daniel Huet, der Cardinal Heinrich Noris, Anton Pagi, Samuel Basnage, Claude Fleury, die Brüder Peter und Hieronymus Ballerini, Natalis Alexander, Remy Ceillier an.

**English** (Own translation.)

> Caesar Baronius held that, given the state of the synodal Acts, the testimony of the ancient tradition was quite sufficient to prove with certainty that the fifth general synod had condemned Origen and his teachings after separate deliberations. To name only a few eminent representatives of this view, he was followed, more or less without reserve, by Severinus Binius, Pierre de Marca, Pierre-Daniel Huet, Cardinal Enrico Noris, Antoine Pagi, Samuel Basnage, Claude Fleury, the brothers Pietro and Girolamo Ballerini, Natalis Alexander and Remy Ceillier.

**Edition:** F. Diekamp, Die origenistischen Streitigkeiten (Münster i. W.: Aschendorff, 1899), Einleitung, p. 2 (page approximate). The works it lists, as Diekamp cites them: P. D. Huet, Origeniana (Rouen, 1668); H. Noris, Dissertatio historica de Synodo quinta (Padua, 1673, with the Historia Pelagiana); P. and H. Ballerini, Defensio dissertationis Norisianae de Synodo V. adversus dissertationem P. Garnerii, in Noris, Opera omnia IV (Verona, 1732).  
**Checked against:** archive.org scan of the 1899 printing (OCR text), item dieorigenistisch00diek. ([source 1](https://dn790008.ca.archive.org/0/items/dieorigenistisch00diek/dieorigenistisch00diek.pdf), [source 2](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm))

**About the text:** OCR corrections made: '(lüs;ir' read as 'Cäsar', 'hieli' as 'hielt', 'dafs' as 'daß'. The camp held more than that the name in canon 11 was genuine. ### 15. Hefele's verdict ('mere conjecture') and the NPNF editor's ('no possible reason to doubt')

**Source:** K. J. von Hefele, A History of the Councils of the Church IV (Eng. trans. 1895), p. 336, note on canon XI of 553, reprinted in NPNF II.14; H. R. Percival, NPNF II.14, Excursus on the XV Anathemas (1900) · **Date:** 1875 (German, 2nd ed.); 1895 (English); 1900 (NPNF)  
**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

This is the classic answer to the interpolation case and the place most later writers quote. Hefele gives the four arguments for removing the name, then his replies and his three reasons for keeping it. Percival adds the point that matters most for the doctrinal debate: even if the fifteen anathemas came from an earlier synod, a genuine name in canon 11 adopts that synod's verdict.

**English (W. R. Clark's 1895 translation of Hefele's German; Percival wrote in English)**

> [Hefele:] Halloix, Garnier, Basnage, Walch and others suppose, and Vincenzi maintains with great zeal, that the name of Origen is a later insertion in this anathematism, because […] (c) because this anathematism is identical with the tenth in the omologia of the Emperor, […] but in the latter the name of Origen is lacking […] All these considerations seem to me of insufficient strength, or mere conjecture, to make an alteration in the text and arbitrarily to remove the name of Origen. […] Finally, only on the ground that the name of Origen really stood in the eleventh anathematism, can we explain the widely-circulated ancient rumour that our Synod anathematized Origen and the Origenists.
[Percival:] That Origen was condemned by name in the Eleventh Canon of this council there seems no possible reason to doubt. But even if these anathemas were adopted at the Home Synod before the meeting of the Fifth Ecumenical, it is clear that by including his name among those of the heretics in the XIth Canon, it practically ratified and made its own the action of that Synod.

**English** (Public domain (1895 and 1900).)

> Original is English (see above).

**Edition:** Hefele, Conciliengeschichte II, 2nd ed. (Freiburg, 1875); Diekamp cites pp. 790 ff., 859 ff., 898 f. for Hefele's treatment, and the exact page of this note in the German was not confirmed. English: A History of the Councils of the Church IV, trans. W. R. Clark (Edinburgh, 1895), p. 336 (as cited in NPNF). NPNF II.14 (New York, 1900). Schaff, History of the Christian Church III (rev. 1884), § 144, cites 'Hefele (ii. p. 874)' for the same view (edition of Hefele not stated).  
**Checked against:** NPNF II.14 as transcribed at tertullian.org and CCEL. The German original was not checked. ([source 1](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm), [source 2](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.vii.html), [source 3](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf214.xii.viii.html))

### 16. Diekamp (1899): the mediating view and the new reconstruction

**How far it was checked:** V, checked against an edition or a scan of one.

It records the state of the question in 1899: the interpolation thesis was by then a minority view, and Hefele's position, that the name is genuine, was the majority view among scholars. Diekamp then offered the reconstruction most historians still use, which places a real condemnation of Origenism by the same bishops just before the council opened. That reconstruction is about the fifteen anathemas; the step from there to the name's place in canon 11 is made by Price and Kimel, and was not read in Diekamp's own chapter.

> Weitaus die meisten Gelehrten unserer Tage treten der in der älteren Zeit durch Heinrich Valesius und Jakob Basnage aufgestellten, neuerdings durch Carl Joseph v. […] Wenn ich eine neue Behandlung der verwickelten und schwierigen Frage in Angriff zu nehmen wage, so geschieht es, weil ich glaube, daß eine von Grund auf neue Untersuchung der Quellen und insbesondere die Ausnutzung einiger bisher gar nicht verwertheter oder nicht ausreichend gewürdigter Documente uns der richtigen Lösung um ein Erhebliches näher zu bringen vermag.

**English** (Own translation.)

> By far the majority of scholars today hold the mediating view, put forward long ago by Henri de Valois and Jacques Basnage and recently grounded with care by Carl Joseph von Hefele. […] If I venture to take up this tangled and difficult question afresh, it is because I believe that a fundamentally new study of the sources, and in particular the use of some documents not used at all so far or not adequately weighed, can bring us considerably nearer to the right solution.

### 17. Crouzel: Origen missing from the emperor's speech and from Vigilius's letter

> Original is English.

(*name genuine*; read in the scholar's own words; [source](https://orthodoxchristiantheology.com/2021/02/06/responses-to-father-kimels-universalist-reblog/))

## Other things the check turned up

- PLACE BY PLACE, how secure is Origen's name? 400 synod (Theophilus) and Pope Anastasius: present and secure as to the name (named repeatedly; the texts survive in Jerome's correspondence, in one 13th-century manuscript). The Jerusalem bishops' reply (Ep. 93) anathematizes the listed propositions and whoever preaches them without naming Origen. 543 edict: present (Greek and Migne's Latin agree on 'Origen, who is also called Adamantius'); only Vincenzi denied the edict. 551 Confession of the Right Faith: securely absent. 553 Justinian's letter to the bishops: present (date disputed, 543 or spring 553). 553 fifteen anathemas: absent from every anathema and from the manuscript heading; Origen is in modern titles only. 553 session V: present in the Latin Acts (English only read here); challenged by Halloix and Vincenzi; what 'even now' refers to is disputed. 553 canon 11: present in the Latin Acts and in the Greek canons; no manuscript without it is reported; doubted on inference only. Vigilius 553-554: absent (the letter to Eutychius has no list of heresiarchs at all; Constitutum II not checked). Gregory the Great's letters on the councils: absent. Cyril c. 557 and Evagrius Scholasticus c. 593: present, in partisan or later narratives. Lateran 649, Trullo 692, Constantinople III 681 and Nicaea II 787: present, with Didymus and Evagrius. *Source:* This cluster's passages; see each passage's sources. (from a reliable report of the source's view)
- NPNF's English of the 543 closing anathema ('Anathema to Origen and to that Adamantius') splits one person into two. The Greek τῷ καὶ Ἀδαμαντίῳ and Migne's Latin 'qui et Adamantius' both mean 'Origen, also called Adamantius'. Where the versions differ is in the rest of the sentence. NPNF cites Labbe and Cossart as its source, so the editor's note ('the Latin and Greek do not agree') may describe a printing I could not see. *Source:* PG 86.1, cols. 989-990 (OGL OCR); NPNF II.14 (read in the source's own words)
- Canon 11 of 553 differs from the Homologia's tenth anathema in more than Origen's name. It adds 'with their impious writings', the clause on heretics condemned by the four councils, and 'persisted in their impiety until death'. So canon 11 is a longer, reworked text. That fits Hefele's suggestion that the emperor or the bishops added Origen at the council, and it also fits an interpolator who started from the 551 list; the differences do not settle which. *Source:* PG 86.1 col. 1017-1018 compared with DH 433 and the Greek text (read in the source's own words)
- The argument that Origen's last place in canon 11 is 'out of chronological order' is weakened by the Lateran list of 649, which is not chronological either (Paul of Samosata follows Severus and Theodosius). That shows such lists were not always compiled by date; it does not show that Origen's place in canon 11 is original. This is my own observation from the texts, not an argument I found in a scholar. *Source:* DH 518-520 (clerus.org) (read in the source's own words)
- Vigilius's letter to Eutychius of 8 December 553 (in the NPNF translation) anathematizes Theodore of Mopsuestia 'together with all other heretics' condemned by the four councils and anathematizes no other heresiarch by name (Nestorius and Apollinaris are named only in descriptions of what Theodoret and Ibas wrote or defended). Its silence about Origen is therefore not a deliberate omission from a parallel list. *Source:* NPNF II.14, 'The Decretal Letter of Pope Vigilius' (read in the source's own words)

- ARGUMENT, chronological order. For: Origen (died c. 254) comes after Eutyches (condemned 451) in a list otherwise in date order, which suggests the name was added (Ramelli 2019; Crouzel 1999; Schaff 1884 calls the connection 'incongruous'). Halloix's version, as Garnier reports it: some Acephalus put Origen in place of Dioscorus, who would have closed the list in order. Against: the list keeps the order in which each man was anathematized, and the council is 'only adding Origen last because he was not in the edict' (Truglia 2021); Origen, condemned in 543 or by the bishops just before the council, naturally comes last (Kimel comment 27 Dec 2021; Price 2009, vol. 2, p. 123 n. 86, as quoted by Baghos 2015). *Source:* Ramelli 2019 (Brill PDF); Alura's translation of Crouzel 1999; Schaff HCC III §144; Garnier, PL 68.1075C; Truglia 2021; Kimel comment 27 Dec 2021; Baghos 2015 quoting Price (read in the source's own words)
- ARGUMENT, the 551 confession of faith. For: Justinian's 551 confession of faith against the Three Chapters (ὁμολογία; Latin 'Confessio rectae fidei') has a matching tenth anathema listing the same heretics without Origen (Hefele's reason c; Crouzel, whom Alura glosses as 'Homonoia/Confessio rectae fidei', number 10; Ramelli, who calls it the Homonoia; Harding, who calls the Homonoia the chancery's first draft of the anathemas). Against: Hefele says the emperor or the bishops may simply have gone further two years later. Truglia says a 551 document on the Three Chapters would not be expected to name Origen, and that expecting it is anachronism. *Source:* Hefele, History of the Councils IV p. 336 (NPNF reprint); Truglia 2021; Ramelli 2019; Wikipedia 'Origenist crises' (Harding 2004) (read in the source's own words)

Hefele calls it the emperor's 'omologia' (confession) and says its tenth anathema matches canon 11; Crouzel's 'corresponding list', no. 10, is glossed by Alura as 'Homonoia/Confessio rectae fidei'. Harding (via Wikipedia, citing her p. 166, note 20) calls the Homonoia 'the first draft of the anathemata issued by the imperial chancery'. Kimel (2026, part 3, n. 3) quotes McGuckin: 'a letter (homonoia) seems to have been issued by the emperor's personal cabinet to the assembled bishops'; Kimel elsewhere speaks of 'the original homonoia' as differing from the received canon. These may be one document or three. Hefele's and Crouzel's reading (the 551 Confession) is the only one for which I found the text: PG 86.1, col. 1017-1018, tenth anathema, which lacks Origen. If the Homonoia is instead a pre-conciliar draft of the 553 canons, the omission would be stronger evidence than the 551 parallel. An earlier note in this cluster called the name 'evidently a slip' for Homologia; that is too firm and has been withdrawn. *Source:* Ramelli 2019; Hefele via NPNF; Kimel 2026 n. 3 and n. 33; Wikipedia 'Origenist crises' (from a reliable report of the source's view)

Against: only the Three Chapters Acts were sent to the pope (Prat 1911); no copy of the anathemas sent to Vigilius survives (Truglia 2021); Vigilius is said to have signed Justinian's 543 edict naming Origen (Price, vol. 1, p. 43, as quoted by Truglia 2019). This is common in reincarnation literature, e.g. Head and Cranston, Reincarnation: The Phoenix Fire Mystery (1977), as quoted in a 2018 Medium post (the post was not re-opened in this check). - ARGUMENT, the principle of the list (the interpolation side's strongest form). For: the six names before Origen are the heretics the four earlier councils condemned, and they fall in the order of those councils (Arius, Nicaea; Eunomius and Macedonius, Constantinople I; Apollinaris, normally placed there; Nestorius, Ephesus; Eutyches, Chalcedon), and the canon adds 'all other heretics condemned ... by the Church and by the aforesaid four councils'. Origen was condemned by none of the four. Halloix's version (PL 68.1075C): the list is of heretics condemned in the four councils, so Origen was put in place of Dioscorus. Hefele lists it among the four reasons ('only such heretics would be named as had been condemned by one of the first four Ecumenical Synods') and calls them 'of insufficient strength, or mere conjecture'. Słomski (2025) gives the order version only. Against: Truglia and Kimel (2021) say the list keeps the order in which each man was anathematized, so Origen, condemned last, stands last; Price (via Baghos) says the last place reflects a real renewed condemnation of Origenism just before the council opened; the omnibus clause says 'by the Church', not only by the councils; the Lateran list of 649 is not in date order either. Gregory the Great's summary (next finding) fits the interpolation side's picture but omits Apollinaris, who is in canon 11. None of these answers shows the name was or was not original. *Source:* Garnier PL 68.1075C via https://la.m.wikisource.org/wiki/Dissertatio_ad_Liberatum (read 2026-10-20); Hefele via https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm (read 2026-10-20); Słomski p. 43; Truglia 2021; Baghos 2015 quoting Price (read in the source's own words)
- Gregory the Great, Epistle XXV (NPNF II.12, to John of Constantinople and the other patriarchs; the page gives no date): 'I confess that I receive and revere, as the four books of the Gospel so also the four Councils: to wit, the Nicene, in which the perverse doctrine of Arius is overthrown; the Constantinopolitan also, in which the error of Eunomius and Macedonius is refuted; further, the first Ephesine, in which the impiety of Nestorius is condemned; and the Chalcedonian, in which the pravity of Eutyches and Dioscorus is reprobated.' Of the fifth council he says: 'The fifth council also I equally venerate, in which the epistle which is called that of Ibas, full of error, is reprobated; Theodorus ... is convicted of having fallen into the perfidy of impiety; and the writings of Theodoritus ... are refuted'. Neither Origen nor Apollinaris is named on the page. No scholar I read ties this letter to canon 11; the parallel with the list is the interpolation advocate's own observation. - Hefele's four reasons and his replies, read at tertullian.org on 2026-10-20 (Hist. Councils IV, p. 336, in NPNF II.14, note on Capitulum XI): (a) Theodore Ascidas, 'the Origenist, was one of the most influential members of the Synod'; (b) 'only such heretics would be named as had been condemned by one of the first four Ecumenical Synods'; (c) the anathema 'is identical with the tenth in the omologia of the Emperor', where Origen's name is lacking; (d) Origen's errors differ from those of the group. Replies: 'insufficient strength, or mere conjecture'; the emperor may have 'subsequently went further than in his omologia', or the bishops 'of their own accord, added Origen'; the Roman copy of the Acts was 'probably prepared for Vigilius himself'; the New Lavra monks' break with the signing bishops, and 'the widely-circulated ancient rumour', are explained only if the name stood in canon 11. The advocates' charge that the REPORT leaves out Hefele's own concession that the name is absent from the emperor's 551 text is correct: on his second alternative, the name is an addition by the bishops at the council. *Source:* https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm (read in the source's own words)
- Kimel, 'Apokatastasis, Origen, and the Fifth Ecumenical Council (Part 3)', 9 Jan 2026, as read on 2026-10-20: n. 33, 'This minority view continues to find support among contemporary Church historians' (of the interpolation of Origen's name); n. 3, McGuckin: 'a letter (homonoia) seems to have been issued by the emperor's personal cabinet to the assembled bishops'; n. 16, Whitby: the anti-Origenist proceedings 'preceded the Council and were not regarded as a formal part of proceedings'; n. 10, Hombergen (p. 293): Cyril's 'representation of the facts . . . seriously contradicts the historical evidence'; and in the main text, Price (as Kimel gives it): 'the Latin translation was likely produced soon after the finalization of the Greek text'. - State of the Acts, read 2026-10-20. - ARGUMENT, the cost of the hypothesis. For the name: to drop it one must also reject, or explain away, canon 11, the session V passage (Halloix, Vincenzi), the 543 edict (Vincenzi), and the reports of Cyril and Evagrius; no ancient reader is known to have suspected the name; the case begins with Halloix in 1648, whose motive was to clear Origen (Diekamp: 'eine krasse Tendenzschrift'). McClymond (2015, p. 818 n. 10): the interpolation 'must have been added so quickly to the original text that no one recognized it as an interpolation', and he asks how Ramelli can see it when 'no one before her seems to have done so'. Answer: the claim is old (Halloix, Garnier, Basnage, Walch, Vincenzi, as Hefele, Schaff and Diekamp record), and Ramelli replies that it 'was certainly not discovered by me'; the hypothesis is of an early insertion, so its being unnoticed later is expected. Limit: Diekamp's and Harnack's remarks on Halloix and Vincenzi are judgments of motive, not refutations; and an insertion made before Cyril wrote (c. 557) leaves little room. The Catholic Dictionary, as the NPNF page cites it, calls Vincenzi's attack on the Acts 'unworthy of serious notice'. *Source:* McClymond and Ramelli reply (passage ramelli-mcclymond); Diekamp (passage halloix-1648); https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-110.htm (read in the source's own words)
- ARGUMENT, the bridge from the name to the council's intent. Percival (NPNF II.14, excursus): even if the fifteen anathemas were adopted at the home synod, 'by including his name among those of the heretics in the XIth Canon, it practically ratified and made its own the action of that Synod'. Justinian's covering letter asks the bishops to anathematize each chapter 'together with the impious Origen', so the target was never in doubt, as even Kimel concedes (2026, part 4, n. 8). Limits: Kimel and Oxenham distinguish condemning Origen 'generally' from condemning every doctrine he taught; Truglia withdrew his earlier claim that canon 11 explicitly cites the 543 anathemas; and this argument assumes the name is genuine. *Source:* https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-115.htm (read 2026-10-20); Kimel part 4 https://afkimel.wordpress.com/2026/01/12/apokatastasis-origen-and-the-fifth-ecumenical-council-part-4/ (read in the source's own words)
- ARGUMENT, motive and timing. The interpolation side: a party that already claimed the council's authority for its anathema had the motive, and the same milieu attached the fifteen anathemas to the 'fifth synod' by heading; Ramelli reports that the Sabaites 'maintained that the council issued a definitive anathema against Origen'; McGuckin (2017, as Kimel reports) holds that the fifteen were interpolated into the Greek Acts. Hart states the thesis flatly and gives no evidence in that essay. The genuine-name side: Cyril's account is a partisan's (Hombergen, as Kimel quotes him), which cuts both ways, since the earliest report of a conciliar anathema is also a partisan's; the question of the fifteen anathemas is a different question from the name in canon 11, and popular writing (Hart 2015) often runs them together. *Source:* Ramelli PDF (above); Kimel part 3 notes 3, 10, 33; Hart https://firstthings.com/saint-origen/ (read in the source's own words)
- ARGUMENT, the monks' reaction and the 'ancient rumour'. Hefele reads the break of the New Lavra monks with the bishops who signed the Acts, and the old report that the council anathematized Origen, as explained only by the name in canon 11 (V, tertullian.org). A Wikipedia article on Eustochius of Jerusalem says that at a Jerusalem synod 'all the bishops of Palestine, except for Alexander of Abila, confirmed the Fifth Council's verdicts' and that in 555 Eustochius 'drove some sixty monks' from the New Lavra; that article cites nothing for either sentence (S). Against: the reports come from Cyril, whom Hombergen and Kimel judge partisan or compressing, and the reaction would also follow if the pre-conciliar anti-Origenist proceedings (Whitby) were attributed to the council. *Source:* https://tertullian.org/fathers2/NPNF2-14/Npnf2-14-114.htm; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustochius_of_Jerusalem (read 2026-10-20) (from a reliable report of the source's view)
- WEIGHT OF SCHOLARSHIP. Diekamp (1899, Einleitung): by far most scholars of his day held Hefele's view that the council named Origen expressly, 'in passing and in a heap' (V, from the archive.org scan). Kimel (2026, part 3, n. 33) calls interpolation a 'minority view' that 'continues to find support among contemporary Church historians' (V). Alura (2021) calls it 'serious and respectable' and 'a reasonable conclusion', with 'no smoking gun' (V). Hold the name genuine: Hefele, Percival, Diekamp's majority, Crouzel (who accepts the name), Price, McClymond, Truglia, Kimel. Hold it inserted or possibly inserted: Halloix, Garnier (as reported), Basnage, Walch, Vincenzi, Ramelli, Harding (via Wikipedia), Hart, Słomski. No survey of present specialist opinion was found, so how the balance stands today is not known. *Source:* Diekamp via archive.org item dieorigenistisch00diek; Kimel part 3; Alura (shamelessorthodoxy.com) (read in the source's own words)
- Crouzel's position, clarified. In the New Catholic Encyclopedia he writes that 'in its discussions little attention was paid to Origen, except to put his name in the list of heretics condemned in canon 11', and that Origen was not mentioned in the emperor's opening discourse, 'the source of the council's anathemas'. In 1999 (Alura's translation) he notes the name's last place 'although the other names are ranked by chronological order', asks 'What is the sense of this addition', and answers 'No' to whether Origen is condemned by the council as a matter of the canonical texts' own wording. So he accepts the name as present, records the anomaly, and doubts the condemnation's force and content. The REPORT's sentence that Crouzel 'argued that Origen was never properly condemned at all' reads as if he denied the name. *Source:* https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/origen-and-origenism; https://shamelessorthodoxy.com/2021/01/21/orthodoxy-universal-salvation-are-the-two-compatible/ (read in the source's own words)
