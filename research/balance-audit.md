# Balance audit

Done 2026-10-15, on REPORT.md as drafted. The test: would a careful reader on each side recognise their own case, stated at its strongest, and would they find the framing loaded against them?

## Findings and what was done about them

1. **The universalist side has more voices.** That partly reflects the literature, where patristic specialists since Guillaumont have mostly separated Origen from Origenism. But it also reflects the reader's starting point (Kimel's letter). **Done:** section 5 states the "yes" case first and on its own terms, with its strongest evidence (the 543 ninth anathema needs no pre-existence) up front. Section 3 calls the 543 text "the plainest condemnation of universal restoration in the sources".
2. **Jenkins is heard through Kimel.** That is a real limit, and it's stated in the report's gaps paragraph and in claims.md (#54). **Recommended:** watch the Ancient Faith interview and replace the reported summary with his own words before publishing.
3. **Loaded wording checked.** "Monstrous restoration" and "fabulous pre-existence" are quoted from the anathemas, not used as the report's own words. "Politics" appears in section 7 under "against", with the "for" case given equal space. The report doesn't call any position "the consensus" without naming who holds it, except Diekamp's dating, where Price and Wikipedia both call it the standard view.
4. **Canon 11 draft overstated one side.** The draft said most historians accept Origen's name as original. That couldn't be supported, so it was cut (claims.md, corrections).
5. **Tradition labels.** Orthodox opinion is shown as divided, not as one bloc on either side. The Catholic paragraph reports what the edition and Benedict XVI say without inferring a Catholic position on universalism.
6. **Justification section.** Kept as arguments for and against, with no verdict. Richardson's line is quoted as his, not adopted.

## Residual risk

- Several **R** claims (Guillaumont, Edwards, Clark) rest on standard summaries. If they are misstated, the error favours the "Origen not condemned" side, since those works carry that case. Check them first.
- Hart's position is known here only through a critic (Truglia). Same remedy as Jenkins.

## Second pass (2026-10-15)

- **Hart** is now quoted from his own essay ("Saint Origen", *First Things*, 2015). His claim that the fifteen were attached after the council is set against Diekamp and Price, so the strongest form of his case doesn't stand unanswered.
- **Jenkins**: credentials confirmed (PhD; St Basil Center, Eastern University). The interview isn't online among his podcast episodes, so his own words still can't be quoted. This stays the main open limit, and it's on the side the reader is less inclined toward. The artifact should say "as reported by Kimel" whenever it gives his view.
- **Edwards**: the stronger form of his view (Origen didn't teach pre-existence at all) is now stated, and marked as a minority position, so it doesn't read as consensus.
- **Guillaumont**: the draft's "often word for word" overstated what could be checked and is cut. Misiarczyk's dissent is now answered by the majority view that the Greek fragments favour S2.
- **New voices on the traditional side** (Green, Maximov, Telea) were added from Kimel's 2026 series, which narrows the imbalance noted in finding 1.

**Verdict after the second pass:** ready for the artifact, with Jenkins quoted only as reported.
